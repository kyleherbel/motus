# Example 1: a tool explainer with a comment call to action

A worked plan for a typical video of the style: a hook naming a new tool, how it works in three steps, a comparison, its features, and a comment call to action. It is the plan used for the style's end-to-end test. The take is one of the repository owner's own Daily Update recordings (84.7 s of 3840x2160, cut to 49.5 s), so the words are real speech. The plan validates against the style (`motus validate`) and renders.

Files: `01-tool-explainer.words.json` (the conformed words the plan points at) and `01-tool-explainer.edit.json` (the plan).

## The take

A new free GitHub repo (OpenReply) that replaces a paid comment-to-DM tool; how it works (pick a keyword, give a resource away); what it saves (no per-contact fee); extra requirements (follow-gating, DM flows); a verdict; "comment the word reply … and I'll send you the link".

Two transcript repairs came first (STYLE.md section 3):

- The recogniser had dropped three repeated takes and stretched a neighbouring word over each gap. "Minichat." lasted 3.8 s, "keyword" 5 s and "requirements," 2.6 s, with no silence inside any of them. Re-transcribing those stretches with conditioning off showed the opening line, the "you basically pick … keyword" line and the "there's also the ability to have specific …" line each said twice. The cuts keep the last take of each.
- One false start ("this is going to fundamentally change the…") is cut, and the product name is fixed to "ManyChat" (a second recognition pass heard it that way).

## Reading it

1. **Anchors.** The hook is sentence 1 (4.2 s). The name lands in sentence 2, "It's called OpenReply." This is the first cut to full height, at sentence 2 as in 17 of 32 reference reels, with the name as the serif concept caption [S12, T12]. The call to action is the last sentence [S15, H23].
2. **Title.** "The Free GitHub Repo / That Kills *ManyChat*": 4 + 3 words, Title Case, not first person, the comparison word in the serif. A bar sits on line 2 [H25, T17].
3. **Something lands every second or two.** Every panel stretch has beats on the spoken words (STYLE.md 6.1):
   - the hook's three claims join as chips (free-to-download, GitHub, kills);
   - "how it works" is a numbered list whose highlight steps down on "pick", "represent" and "give";
   - his own comment CTA ("comment the word blank") types into a glowing script card and lights its CTA lines;
   - the price point is a before-and-after pair, each side on its words;
   - the three requirements fill a table row by row.
4. **Cut-ins.** Four full-height runs:
   - the name (1.7 s);
   - "I do this all the time in my own videos" (1.6 s);
   - the contrast "however, you don't have to pay every single time…" (3.7 s, push-in);
   - the verdict plus the lead-in to the call to action (7.6 s, larger push-in).

   Full-height share 0.30, 9.7 switches a minute, first cut at 4.2 s [S02-S05]. The title "How OpenReply Works" comes back complete after the cut-in [M09].
5. **No faked captures.** No capture of the repo was available, so every graphic is built from words he says [G20].
6. **The close.** The edit cuts to the light field on "comment". The lockup "Comment 'Reply' / To Get The Link" builds 2 frames after the word and holds to the last frame [H19, H20, L22]. The caption reads `Comment “Reply”` [T13].

## Options left for the user

- s09: the table, or a highlighted list.
- s11: light or dark field for the call to action.
