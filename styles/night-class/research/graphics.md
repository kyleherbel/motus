# Graphics

Measured the graphics of 32 in-style clips (24 dark, 7 mixed light/dark, 1 light; 2880.6 s, 70,005 frames at 23.976/24/25/30 fps). Every frame was decoded with a calibrated BT.709 limited-to-full conversion (white test patch Y=234 -> #fefefe, black field Y=16 -> #000000) and classified into split layout (graphic field over the presenter panel), graphic full-frame, or presenter full-frame. The graphic stretches were cut into 1022 candidate units by hard cuts, drift and layout changes, and every unit was labelled by eye with 23 component codes. The 1218 ASR clauses were categorised by keyword rules and mapped to the graphic on screen ). Recurring rebuilt components were measured on native-resolution frames: title bar, document card, step rows, outlined chips, tier bars, bracket headers, chart, icon nodes, phone frame, capture frames, CTA page grid, and the light-field cards.

A graphic is on screen 70.5% of the runtime (58-84%). A new graphic unit arrives about 16 times a minute (unit median 1.96 s) and the editor cuts back to the full-height presenter every 4.9 s for about 3.1 s. Graphic onsets fall on clause starts 2.4 times more often than chance and peak on the word-onset frame. The step-row list (dark rows, red left bar, white index, unspoken rows blurred) is the workhorse at 31% of graphic time. Captures the style cannot draw (UI screenshots, thumbnails, video clips, profiles, logos, document pages) fill a median 54% of graphic time, so every video needs about 12 supplied media items. A two-line title lockup with a sharp red bar (#bc0102) is pinned at the top for 63% of graphic time. Everything is centred on x=540 inside about x 120-960, y 300-1240.

Over time the later generation (Oct 24 - Nov 11, 17 clips) doubles the share of step-row lists (20% -> 42%) and phones/video captures, halves logos, node and metaphor diagrams, changes graphics a little more slowly (18.3 -> 14.2 units/min) and adds the light field (8 of 17 clips). Not done: no motion curves (timing of entrances was not measured), no listening, fonts not identified, clause categories are automatic (33/40 agree with a human spot check), unit labels were read from small tiles (45/48 right in a spot check).

## Rules

Each rule: what to build, the numbers (median, range, n), the evidence (clip@frame), and the confidence.

### [G01] Keep a graphic on screen for about 70% of the runtime; the rest is the full-height presenter with captions only.

- Values: Graphic share of runtime per clip: median 70.5%, range 58.2-83.9% (n=32 clips, 2880.6 s, 70,005 frames, every frame classified). Dark family median 70.9% (n=24), mixed 69.7% (n=7), light 68.0% (n=1). Split layout (field + presenter panel) median 69.8%; graphic full-frame with the panel hidden median 0%, present above 1% in 9 clips, max 42.0% (clip33).
- Evidence: n=32 clips, every frame., State spot check on 48 random frames: 48/48 correct ).
- Confidence: high

### [G02] Change the graphic about every 2 s and cut back to the presenter every 5 s or so: build runs of 2-3 graphic units between short presenter cut-ins.

- Values: Graphic units per minute: median 16.3, range 7.6-25.7 (n=32). Graphic unit duration: median 1.96 s, p10 0.38 s, p90 5.67 s (n=763 units). - Evidence: n=32 clips, 1022 candidate units labelled by eye.,.
- Confidence: high (durations depend on the unit cutter: hard cut >14, drift >25; a slow build inside one card counts as one unit)

### [G03] Start a new graphic on the first word of a clause, on that word's onset frame.

- Values: Graphic unit onsets within +-6 frames of a clause start: 49.4% vs 20.5% for random times (n=731 onsets). - Evidence: n=731 onsets in 32 clips;, sync. ASR timings are estimates (not forced-aligned), so the true alignment is probably tighter.
- Confidence: medium (unverified ASR word times)
- Verified, confirmed (the other study's value): Offsets in frames at each clip's own rate, from the onset frame to the nearest transcript word start (ASR word timing). Hard graphic-to-graphic swaps (single-frame change inside the graphic layout): G2 n=42 in 14 clips, median -0.5 frame, |offset| median 1.28, 40 % within 1 frame, 71 % within 2, 88 % within 3. All 32 clips: n=114 in 27 clips, median 0.0, 53 % within 1, 88 % within 3. Cuts from presenter to graphic layout: G2 n=125 in 17 clips, median -0.13 frame, 57 % within 1, 93 % within 3; all n=235, median -0.16, 92 % within 3. Motion onsets (first changed frame of animated runs): G2 n=366 in 17 clips, median +0.30 frame, 40 % within 1, 82 % within 3; all n=724, median +0.28, 82 % within 3. Chance (random graphic-layout frames): 30 % within 1, 74 % within 3, |offset| median 1.76 frames. Nearest clause-initial word (first word, after punctuation, or after a >=0.25 s gap): swaps 50 % within 3 frames (G2), 53 % (all); motion onsets 40 %; cuts to graphic 75 %. S18 holds (88 % vs 83 %). Changes do sit on word starts (median offset -0.5 to +0.3 frame), but the 3-frame window is weak evidence because words are dense: 74 % of random frames also fall within 3 frames of a word start. The 1-frame rate (40-57 % vs 30 % chance) is the real signal. G03's median offset is reproduced (-0.13 to +0.3 frame), but 'first word of a clause' is not: only 50-53 % of swaps are within 3 frames of a clause-initial word. Word timings are ASR estimates, not forced alignment, so the 1-frame figures carry about +/-2 frames of timing error. Builder: start the change on the onset frame of the word it illustrates, with no lead or lag; a clause boundary is not required..
