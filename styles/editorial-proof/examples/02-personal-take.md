# Example 2: a personal take with nothing to capture

The hard case: one person, one lesson, no product, no page, no number on any website, and a call to action that is not "comment". The grammar does not change; the cards restate the speaker's own words (section 6.6). The transcript is invented. The plan validates against the style (`motus validate`) on a copy of a test project.

Files: `02-personal-take.words.json` (the conformed words) and `02-personal-take.edit.json` (the plan, also printed at the end).

## The transcript (151 words, 39.2 s, 231 words a minute)

> I stopped posting every single day, and my views actually went up. For two years I posted daily, and honestly, most of those videos were rushed. I was writing at midnight, filming in one take, and just hoping something would stick. Every single video started to feel like homework. So last month I tried the opposite. Two videos a week, and each one gets a full day of work. One day to write it, and one day to film and edit it properly, and nothing goes out until it's ready. The result surprised me. My average views tripled, the comments got longer, and I'm spending fewer hours on content than I did before. Posting less forced me to only make the ideas I actually believe in, and that's what people share. If you want the exact weekly schedule I use, join my free school community. It's pinned right at the top.

Transcription suspect: "school" (word 143, p 0.58). In context it is the community platform Skool; confirm with the user, then fix it. (It never shows: the CTA block replaces the captions from "join" on.)

## Reading it

1. **Kind of video.** No ordinals, no tool, no page: a single-subject personal take. The beats are the speaker's: hook (the result), the old habit, the switch, the new routine, the results, the reason, the CTA [S19]. No white interstitials: only list videos get numbered ones, and there is no product to name [H16, S20].
2. **Anchors.** The hook is the first sentence (words 0-11, 96 f) [S02]. The CTA is the last two sentences (131-150, 164 f, inside 80-167 f) [S01]. It is a "join my community" CTA: the ending keeps its form with the speaker's own verb and noun, `verb: "join"` on word 140 and `keyword: "Community"` on word 144, and a captions-only alternative, since he does not ask for a comment (section 8).
3. **Title.** "I Stopped Posting / Every Single Day" (the habit) or "My Views Went Up / When I Stopped" (the result): 3 + 3 and 4 + 3 words, all but "When" spoken in the first sentence [H14, H15].
4. **Hook proof.** The result and the stake side by side: daily posting crossed, views up [G27].
5. **What each sentence gets** (section 6.6, nothing capturable): every number becomes a `big-number` on its word ("two years", "a full day", "tripled" as 3x); every list of habits or steps becomes a `steps-card` whose rows land on their words and are struck or checked as he says so (the old routine struck on "homework"; the new week checked on "ready"; the closing argument as a cause-and-effect chain); every contrast becomes `compare-panels` (habit against result; every day against two a week; comments against hours). Three designed types, none in more than half the card scenes [G21]. Numbers are restatements of his words, never new figures.
6. **Layouts.** Light and dark alternate by sentence (L D P L D L P D L D P). Two punch-ins, the most the style allows: the admission "most of those videos were rushed" (from dark) and the setup line "The result surprised me" (from light), each ending on its sentence end, like the reference's "here's how" [S16]. More are offered at s05, s06 and s10 but not picked: with this long CTA a third would push the full-frame share past 23 %.
7. **No empty stage.** A `big-number` appears on its `at` word, so a scene whose only card is a number starts on that word or one word before it: "a full day of work" (s08) starts on "a", and the 3x (s12) starts rolling on "My" and lands on "tripled". A first draft that started those scenes a sentence earlier left the dark stage empty for up to 2 s; the contact sheet caught it.
8. **Continuity.** A card that keeps being talked about keeps its `key`: the old routine (s04-s05, struck in s05), the switch (s06-s07, its right side landing on "two videos a week"), the new week (s09-s10, checked on "ready") [M12].
9. **Captions.** "two" becomes "2" twice (counts as digits; "one day" stays a word) [T19]. No hidden ranges: no interstitial repeats any words.

## The options

- Two options per scene, each a different idea: the habit against the result in the hook; a number alone against the same number with the presenter; a list against panels; a close reading against a punch-in.
- The CTA has two options because the speaker's call to action is not a comment: `join` + "Community" in the ending block, or the presenter with captions only.
- No option creates an illegal sequence; the presenter is never away for more than about 5 s whatever is picked.

## What to tell the user

- Nothing here could be captured, so every card restates your own words; if you have a screenshot of your analytics or of the community page, it can replace a card (a `media-card` or a `source-card` with `chrome: "none"`).
- "school" read as the platform Skool: confirm.
- The style expects a "comment KEYWORD" ending; this one uses your words ("join", "Community").
- Music bed `b` (the previous example used `a`).
