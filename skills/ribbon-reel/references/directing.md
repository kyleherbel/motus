# Directing with a flexible scene library

Preserve the recognizable material and layer relationships. Adapt the narrative mechanics.

| Spoken content | Useful treatment | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Personal story, qualification, doubt | `clean`, or `depth` with a quiet existing subject card | An abstract cutaway that removes the speaker's expression |
| Subject plus a few attributes | Rear `glass-card`, word-timed rows | A separate full-frame scene for every adjective |
| An ordered process | `abstract` + `glass-stack`; connect when the next step is spoken | Connections that imply an unstated causal relationship |
| Two actual alternatives | `glass-compare`, with focus on the named side | Fabricated before/after values |
| A query or finding an answer | `search-query` with start/completion anchors | Typing generic filler just to make something move |
| Work samples, a product, evidence | Supplied assets in `media-fan` or `filmstrip` | Generated screenshots presented as proof |
| Spoken action | `action-button`, a single click on the action word | A CTA the speaker never made |
| A number, price or duration | `stat` (counts up) behind the head or on the stage | A number the speaker did not say |
| Stop X / start Y, myth / fact | `swap` | Inventing the "old way" |
| Growth or decline over time | `chart` (illustrative heights, spoken labels only) | Axis numbers nobody said |
| A system or toolkit | `orbit` | More satellites than were named |
| A conversation, comment or DM | `chat` in a `split` panel | Paraphrasing into new claims |
| A result, alert or notification | `notify` | A fake brand or app name |
| A rule or quote | `quote` | Attributing words to someone who did not say them |
| One word that carries the line | `big-word` behind the head | A word that reverses the meaning |

## Duration

For a brief 8–15 second thought, two to five scenes may fit; do not impose that count. For 20–45 seconds, group ideas into acts and let an element persist across supporting clauses. For longer footage, reuse the palette, type and material while varying explanation, scale and pacing. A new meaning can earn a new composition. A synonym rarely does. This pack remains 9:16 because that is the Motus output contract.

Keep a real moment of low motion before a major reveal when the delivery supports it. Intensity comes from contrast, not permanent shake. Offer the user a quieter visual option for emotional or dense material.

An abstract scene needs something to read immediately after its transition. Introduce its first object on the opening clause, then use later keyword anchors for the next object or focus change. Inspect the first half-second; avoid cutting away from the speaker into an empty background while waiting for a late keyword. Supporting captions remain visible until a hero phrase enters.

## Variety

A reel is a sequence of different pictures. Change the device every scene, change the composition at least every two scenes, and treat the ribbon as a signature (hook plus one or two moments, different forms), not a background. `examples/03-fitness`, `04-finance` and `05-cooking` show the same grammar on three unrelated niches. Run `dev/audit-plan.mjs` and read its variety warnings before showing stills.

## What is fixed, what changes

Fixed within an edit: fonts and hierarchy, material lighting, the selected visual family, logical layer order, safe copy region. Change per scene: explanatory mechanism, assets, word anchors, duration, evidence emphasis and whether the presenter is visible. Motion setting changes travel/blur/drift but not the spoken anchors.

If the user asks for a new brand color, check whether an installed theme fits. The current renderer declares Orange (default), Violet and Light Lime. For a genuinely different brand, create a deliberate pack variant with corresponding research/token changes; do not put arbitrary values into `look` or promise a color control that does not exist.

## Distinct alternatives

For “three phases,” a stack shows sequence; a rear card keeps the speaker present; a clean shot can work if the explanation is personal. These are different choices. Three identical stacks in different colors are not.

For “more control,” a literal comparison may help only if the speaker names both alternatives. A phrase over the presenter may be more accurate. Preserve modifiers and negation; do not extract a dramatic keyword that reverses the sentence.
